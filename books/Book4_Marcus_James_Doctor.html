Marcus & James
Visit the Doctor

Marcus had a checkup today, and his tummy felt fluttery just thinking about it.
Marcus goes to the doctor.
👩‍⚕️
Dr. Patel
James, who loved facts, started explaining every single step out loud.
James knows facts.
“First they’ll check your ears, then your heart, then—” James went on and on. Marcus’s flutter grew bigger.
Marcus feels bad.

How does Marcus feel?

👩‍⚕️
Dr. Patel
Dr. Patel noticed. “Would it help if you picked the order we do things in?” she asked Marcus.
Marcus can pick.
Marcus thought. “Ears first. Heart last.” Just deciding made the flutter feel smaller.
Marcus picks ears first.
👩‍⚕️
Dr. Patel
Dr. Patel used the stethoscope gently, and Marcus listened to his own heartbeat too.
Marcus taps his chest.
“That wasn’t so bad when I got to choose,” Marcus said.
Marcus is not mad now.

What helped Marcus feel calm?

James promised to ask before explaining next time, and Marcus said that would help a lot.
James will ask first.
🏅

For Grown-Ups

Feeling/skill: Anxiety eases when a child is offered a small, real choice.

Ask your child: What is one choice that would help you feel ready for something new?

Try this: Offer two small choices before your child’s next appointment.

Standards: L.K.1.d, SL.K.2
